Your Soul Existed Before the World. Second Enoch Says So

By Jordan Vale · October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

Your soul was made before the world began. The Second Book of Enoch, also called the Secrets of Enoch or Slavonic Enoch, states that "all souls are prepared to eternity, before the formation of the world." In the text, an angel named Pravuil orders Enoch to write down every human soul that will ever be born. Enoch spends thirty days and thirty nights filling 366 books with the record. The Church left this book out of the Bible, and in 553 AD it formally condemned the teaching that souls exist before birth.

So the book disappeared from the West, and the idea was declared heresy. The manuscripts still exist, in Old Church Slavonic, sitting in Eastern European libraries. They describe a ledger of every person who will ever live, yourself included, written before the first day of creation.

The Book That Survived Only in Slavonic

Second Enoch reached the modern world through one language. Its full text survives only in Old Church Slavonic manuscripts, copied by Orthodox scribes in Russia and the Balkans. Those copies are held today in collections in Moscow and Belgrade. Western Christianity lost the book. The Slavic monasteries kept copying it.

Russian scholar Andrei Popov printed a Slavonic text in 1880. Matvei Sokolov published the long and short versions between 1899 and 1910. In 1896 William Morfill and R. H. Charles brought it into English through Oxford's Clarendon Press under the title The Book of the Secrets of Enoch. That is three publications in thirty years for a book most Bible readers have never heard named.

The text also describes sacrifice as a living practice, which puts its composition in the first century, before Rome destroyed the Jerusalem Temple in 70 AD. That means it circulated at the same time the Gospels were being written. Scholars later identified Coptic fragments of Enoch's heavenly journey recovered at Qasr Ibrim in Nubia. The book travelled from Egypt to Russia, and it never entered your Bible.

Pravuil, the Reed Pen, and the Order

The scene sits in chapters 22 and 23. Enoch is taken up through the heavens and stripped of his earthly clothes. God then calls for an archangel named Pravuil, keeper of the heavenly books. The manuscripts spell his name several ways, including Vretil and Vrevoil, and every version gives him the same job.

Pravuil hands Enoch a reed pen and dictates. Enoch records the movements of the heavens, the elements, the languages, the songs of the angels. Then comes the order that matters. Sit and write all the souls of mankind. Every one that will ever be born.

Enoch writes for thirty days and thirty nights without stopping. When he finishes he has filled 366 books. That number covers one solar year plus a day, a full cycle of time handed back as a census of souls.

"All souls are prepared to eternity, before the formation of the world." Second Enoch, chapter 23.

The Line That Changes Everything

Chapter 23 states it plainly. All souls are prepared to eternity, before the formation of the world. Before light. Before land. Before Adam. Your soul existed before the first day of Genesis, and its name was already on a page.

The book repeats the point later. In chapter 49 God declares that before any man was in his mother's womb, a place was prepared for each soul, along with the measure of how that life would be tested in the world. The soul is waiting before the body is ever formed. The womb becomes a doorway the soul walks through.

Read those two passages together and the picture is complete. Every soul is created first, recorded second, and assigned a place third. Birth is the delivery of something made long before.

Jeremiah Hints. Enoch Shows the Ledger.

Defenders of the canon point to Jeremiah 1:5. "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee." They say the Bible already covers this. Read the verse again. It is one sentence about one prophet, and it says God knew him. It never says Jeremiah's soul existed, where it was kept, or whether yours was there beside it.

Second Enoch answers all three. It says every soul, it says before creation, and it names the archive. Jeremiah gives you a single line. Enoch gives you the whole census, with the angel, the pen and the page count. That is the difference between a hint and a record, and only the hint was allowed into the Bible.

The Church Did More Than Cut the Book

Removing a book was step one. Step two was outlawing the idea itself. The theologian Origen of Alexandria taught in the third century that souls exist before they enter bodies. Three centuries after his death, Emperor Justinian pushed for his condemnation. The anathemas against Origen were tied to the Second Council of Constantinople in 553 AD, and the pre-existence of souls was declared heresy.

Look at the timeline. A first-century book teaches pre-existing souls. A third-century Church father teaches it too. A sixth-century emperor and council ban it. Three centuries, three moves, one teaching erased from Christian belief.

The idea never died where the Church had no authority. The Babylonian Talmud, in tractate Yevamot 62a, speaks of the Guf, a heavenly treasury of souls waiting to be born, and says the Messiah will come only when the Guf is empty. Jewish tradition kept the storehouse. Slavic scribes kept Enoch's ledger. The Western Church kept neither.

What the 366 Books Mean for You

If the ledger is real, your life was written before the world existed. Your soul has an age older than the stars. The question your pastor never answered, where were you before you were born, has an answer in a book sitting in a Moscow library.

That is what makes Second Enoch dangerous to an institution. A soul that existed before creation came from God directly, before any church, sacrament or priest existed to stand in between. You can read the surviving text yourself, along with other books that were cut, in our Lost Books collection, and keep digging in the Vault.

Enoch filled 366 books with souls that had not been born yet. Somebody decided those books were too dangerous for the Bible, and four centuries later somebody decided the idea was too dangerous to believe. Those were two separate decisions, made by two separate authorities, and both came out the same way.

So where are the 366 books now? If every soul was written down before the first day, someone was meant to read that record. And if the Church buried the book and banned the teaching, what exactly did it think readers would find out about where they came from?

Frequently Asked

Does the Bible say your soul existed before birth?

The Bible never says where your soul was before you were born. Jeremiah 1:5 says God knew the prophet before the womb. Second Enoch goes much further and says every soul was prepared before the formation of the world.

What is the Second Book of Enoch?

Second Enoch, also called the Secrets of Enoch or Slavonic Enoch, is a first-century book describing Enoch's journey through the heavens. Its full text survives only in Old Church Slavonic manuscripts, and it was never placed in the Bible.

Who is Pravuil in the Book of Enoch?

Pravuil is the archangel who keeps the heavenly books in Second Enoch. He gives Enoch a reed pen and dictates the record of all creation, including every human soul that will ever be born.

Why did the Church reject the pre-existence of souls?

Pre-existence was taught by Origen of Alexandria and was condemned in anathemas tied to the Second Council of Constantinople in 553 AD under Emperor Justinian. Once it was condemned, a book built on the idea had no route into Christian canon.

How many books did Enoch write about souls?

Second Enoch says he wrote for thirty days and thirty nights and filled 366 books. That number matches one full year plus a day, a complete cycle of time turned into a record of souls.
