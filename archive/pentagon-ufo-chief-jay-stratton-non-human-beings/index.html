The Pentagon's UFO Chief Says He Saw Non-Human Beings Himself

By Jordan Vale · October 20, 2026 · 7 min read

The man the Pentagon paid to hunt UFOs for sixteen years now says he has looked at the beings with his own eyes.

Jay Stratton said it on Fox News, sitting across from Bret Baier, on camera, under his own name. "I have seen non-human beings and non-human craft with my own eyes." One sentence. Eleven words of it are the ones that matter, and he delivered them in the flat voice of a man reading a status report.

The next day his memoir, Out of the Shadows, hit the shelves. This is the career behind that sentence, and the receipts that make it impossible to wave away.

Sixteen Years Inside the Hunt

Jay Stratton is a career intelligence officer who spent sixteen years at the center of the Pentagon's UFO work. He sat in the room when the programs were built. He read the files when they came in. He briefed the people who decided what the public would hear.

His résumé reads like a map of every secret UFO effort the Defense Department has admitted to. He co-founded AAWSAP, the Defense Intelligence Agency program that ran on black-budget money. He then took over as the first director of the Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force, which Deputy Secretary of Defense David Norquist formally stood up in August 2020. Whatever the government knew about these craft, Stratton was the person it reported to.

So when he says he has seen them, he speaks as the chief investigator, with no secondhand witness standing in between. He was the one man in the building with the job of finding out.

AAWSAP, the Program Built to Stay Buried

AAWSAP stands for the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program. It launched in 2008, pushed through by Senate Majority Leader Harry Reid of Nevada, and it ran on roughly twenty-two million dollars of Defense Intelligence Agency money.

The contract went to Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies in Las Vegas. Part of the work centered on Skinwalker Ranch in Utah's Uintah Basin, the most watched patch of ground in American UFO history. Investigators logged craft, lights, and encounters there for years under a federal contract.

The public learned AAWSAP existed only on December 16, 2017, when The New York Times ran its front-page story on the Pentagon's secret UFO program. Nine years of federally funded UFO research, hidden in plain sight. Stratton helped build it from the ground floor.

The Navy Already Showed You the Craft

Stratton's claim lands on top of footage the Pentagon itself certified. Three videos. Three encounters. All three filmed by Navy fighter pilots on their own targeting pods.

FLIR1, recorded in November 2004 by the USS Nimitz carrier strike group off the coast of Southern California, shows the object Commander David Fravor called the Tic Tac. Gimbal and GoFast, recorded in 2015 by jets from the USS Theodore Roosevelt off the East Coast, show objects rotating against the wind and skimming the ocean with no visible propulsion.

On April 27, 2020, the Department of Defense formally released all three and labeled the objects unidentified. The Pentagon put its own seal on the craft. Stratton is now telling you what was flying them.

"I have seen non-human beings and non-human craft with my own eyes." The man who ran the Pentagon's UFO hunt said it on national television, and the Pentagon cleared the book that came out the next day.

A Submarine Used as Bait

The memoir goes further than the interview. Stratton says the United States runs a program to capture these craft and reverse-engineer them. Recovery, study, replication. A full pipeline from crash site to laboratory.

He describes a nuclear submarine used as bait. Think about what that means. The Navy parks one of the most heavily guarded machines on Earth in the water because something keeps coming to look at it. The ocean encounters in the Navy footage stop looking like chance and start looking like a pattern someone learned to exploit.

This lines up with the sworn testimony of David Grusch, the former intelligence officer who told the House Oversight Committee on July 26, 2023 that the government holds recovered craft and non-human biologics. Grusch said it under oath. Stratton, who sat higher in the chain, now says it under his own name.

The Hidden Hand Inside the Pentagon

Stratton's most dangerous claim targets his own building. He says a hidden hand inside the Pentagon told flag officers to stay silent. Admirals and generals, the highest uniformed ranks in the country, instructed to keep what they knew to themselves.

That one claim explains everything else. It explains why Fravor's Tic Tac sat in a drawer for thirteen years. It explains why AAWSAP stayed buried until a newspaper dragged it out. It explains why every congressional hearing ends with officials who cannot discuss the topic in an open setting.

A program that captures non-human craft needs a gatekeeper. Stratton spent sixteen years close enough to see where the gate stands.

Call It a Book Launch

The fastest way to bury Stratton is to call this a book tour. A memoir drops the day after a primetime interview, so the whole thing must be marketing.

That argument collapses on one fact. Every former official who held a security clearance must submit a manuscript to the Defense Office of Prepublication and Security Review before it goes public. The Pentagon read Out of the Shadows before you could. It had the power to strike any line it wanted, and it let this one through.

Then look at what Stratton puts on the table. A top clearance. A federal pension. Sixteen years of reputation. A man selling a lie risks all three for a few months on a bestseller list. A man telling the truth risks them because he has decided the secret costs more than he does.

And the timing cuts the other way. In the very week Stratton went on air, the agency admitted it had lost two hundred forty thousand case files. A quarter of a million records gone, the same week the program's former chief says the evidence walks and breathes. Somebody is clearing shelves while somebody else is finally talking.

Stack the receipts. The Navy filmed the craft. The Pentagon certified the footage. The man who ran the hunt for sixteen years now says he has stood in front of the beings themselves, and the Defense Department cleared his book to say so.

For seventy years the public was told there was nothing up there worth finding. Stratton was the man the government hired to look, and he found something.
