The 13 Maps

  • 1513
    The Piri Reis Map
    Shows a southern coastline matching Antarctica beneath the ice, three centuries before Antarctica was sighted. Compiled from older charts that have never been found.
  • 1570
    Typus Orbis Terrarum
    The opening page of the first modern atlas. Its maker wrote that the Americas were torn away from Africa, three centuries before science agreed.
  • 1569
    The Mercator World Map
    The projection every ship still uses. At the North Pole: a black magnetic mountain, four islands, and a whirlpool that swallowed ships. His source book vanished.
  • 1375
    The Catalan Atlas
    Mansa Musa's gold, Silk Road caravans, and a Mediterranean drawn port by port to a precision nothing in 1375 explains.
  • 4th century
    Tabula Peutingeriana
    Two hundred thousand kilometers of Roman roads on one chart. The timetable of a continent-wide machine, surviving as a single copy.
  • 1915
    The Land Between the Rivers
    Ur, Uruk, Nippur, Babylon, Nineveh. The mounds where the first cities, first writing and first laws appear suddenly, with nothing behind them.
  • circa 1730
    The Holy Land of the Twelve Tribes
    Ten entire tribes deported in 722 BC and never seen again. For centuries, mapmakers kept their territories drawn, waiting.
  • circa 1650
    Aegyptus Antiqua
    Ancient Egypt drawn from Herodotus, who recorded the priests' claim of eleven thousand years of temple records. We file it under legend. He took notes.
  • 1788
    Ancient and Modern Egypt
    Engraved when not one person on Earth could read a hieroglyph. A map of monuments whose builders could no longer be questioned.
  • 1832
    The Empire of Alexander the Great
    Macedonia to the Indus in eleven years. His tomb is still lost inside a city that still exists, after more than 140 official searches.
  • 1154
    Tabula Rogeriana
    The most accurate world map on Earth for three hundred years, drawn with south at the top. Turn it over and the modern world appears.
  • 1801
    Cetus, the Sea Monster
    In the monster's neck sits Mira, a star that vanishes from the sky every 332 days. They drew a beast from the deep around it.
  • 1825
    Perseus and the Head of Medusa
    Medusa's eye is Algol, the Demon Star. An Egyptian papyrus clocked its blink 3,200 years ago, three millennia before astronomy explained it.

Why Maps

A map is a confession. It records exactly what its maker knew, and the moment a map knows something its century should not have known, you have evidence you can hang on a wall. Piri Reis told us himself that he copied older charts. Mercator put a black mountain at the pole because his sources described one. The portolan charts appear in history fully formed, with no crude ancestors. Nobody has explained where that skill came from. These are not decorations. They are documents, and every one of them is an open case.
